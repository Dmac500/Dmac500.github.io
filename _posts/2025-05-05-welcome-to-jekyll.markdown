So here we are once more — starting something new. Time to break things and play until I have a working product that I’m proud to sign my name on. I'm creating this blog to do two things: help other devs, and help myself remember what I’ve been working on.

Before I dive in, here's a bit about me. I'm a software developer at Blue Cross. I work on their Oracle Revenue and Billing Management (ORMB) product — this is how they generate bills and invoices for clients in IL, TX, MT, OK, and NM.
This product isn’t simple. One way I describe it is: imagine a banking software system that’s mostly a black box (I CAN'T SEE THE CODE). A team and I were tasked with molding this into a healthcare solution. We had to learn fast.
I already knew Java and some basic SQL going in, but this product pushed me to grow in many different ways. I quickly learned that data is king, and most issues can be diagnosed by simply following the breadcrumbs.
We also had to constantly adjust to ever-changing third-party technologies like Control-M, Zena, and Databricks — just to name a few — all while trying to align with the company’s continuously shifting goals. I also learned a lot about corporate life along the way.

Growing up, I worked in restaurants, grocery stores, construction, and even as a manager at a movie theater. These were all client-facing jobs, where you learn to change your tone, smile, and be extremely polite.
To the public, we were the nicest, cleanest group of kids — making their experience as enjoyable as possible. But behind closed doors, the façade dropped. It was just a bunch of kids messing around, teasing each other, trying to make $8.25 an hour and have as much fun as they could.
Everything changes when you move into a corporate job. The days of, “Let’s go to Denny’s after work,” are gone. People have lives to live, spouses to see, and kids to raise. We’re more isolated than ever before.
But it’s not all bad — we’re learning how to deliver multi-billion-dollar (yes, with a B) solutions. You learn how to be professional and collaborate with others on a common — and constantly evolving — goal.

With all that said, I honestly don’t know what I want to do with the rest of my career — and that’s okay.
This blog is my way of remembering what I’m working on and sharing it along the way. I don’t yet know what kind of chaos I’m getting myself into.
The only thing left to say, I guess, is...
